# Empathize — evidence and assumptions

**Phase status:** approved by project owner on 2026-11-24  
**Evidence level:** brief-derived facts plus explicit design hypotheses; no learner research has occurred.

## Users and jobs

### Learner — elementary student

- Wants to explore a memorable story and make choices that feel consequential.
- Needs short, concrete examples of saving, spending, and risk.
- Needs kind, retryable explanations rather than a punitive failure state.

### Teacher or caregiver

- May introduce the activity and discuss the learner's reasoning.
- Needs the financial concepts and intended takeaways to be visible and accurate.
- Should be able to use the activity without accounts or setup beyond a browser.

## Needs and risks

- Children need a tangible choice and an understandable explanation for each financial concept.
- Younger readers may need less text and vocabulary support. This is a hypothesis until the grade range is known.
- Investment language can unintentionally imply certainty; feedback must make risk explicit.
- A scary atmosphere can conflict with child comfort. Preserve atmosphere while avoiding gore, threat, or jump scares.
- Repeated multiple-choice patterns may invite guessing. Consider requiring a brief reason, using consequence previews, or changing the interaction format, subject to the game-design tournament.
- Do not collect identifiable child data. If future formal research or data collection is proposed, stop for institutional/guardian review and a new privacy gate.

## Empathy-stage conclusion

The initial brief is sufficient to define a draft problem, but not to claim learner needs are validated. The main gaps are exact age/reading level, use setting, observable learning evidence, and evidence that the visual mood is appropriate. Keep these as open questions and test the riskiest assumptions in a small prototype.
